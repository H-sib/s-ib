H-sib Reminder: PROJECT SUMMARY

1. OVERVIEW

H-sib Reminder is an Android phone app that helps people stop missing deadlines. Most of us hear about dates in a notice, a
group chat or a screenshot, and by the time a date matters it is buried. H-sib Reminder lets you share or paste that message,
finds the dates in it, and shows you what it found and why, so you stay in charge. Nothing is saved as a deadline
until you say yes.

For each deadline it works out the latest day you can start and still finish comfortably, spreads the work over the
days you actually have, and tells you plainly if it does not fit. Then it sends only a few reminders, at sensible
times and never in your quiet hours.

It also has a private space called Close ones for the dates that matter to the people closest to you, such as
anniversaries, birthdays and date nights. It reminds you early enough to plan something, keeps the details off your
lock screen, and keeps them out of everything else the app shows.

If you want, it can also watch the chat apps you pick for messages that mention a deadline and offer them to you for
review, and it can pin a small card in the notification bar showing your next deadline, how many days are left and a
Done button. Both are off until you turn them on, and the app tells you what each one does before it asks.

Your information stays on your phone. The app has no permission to use the internet, and backups are locked with a
key only you hold.

The name is pronounced "sib". It stands for Start It Before, it is the closing sound of the maker's name, Hasib
(which can mean one who counts), and it is also the word for a brother or sister, which is the spirit of Close ones.

2. COMPLEXITY LEVEL

Stack: Kotlin, Jetpack Compose (Material 3 with a custom design system), Room with SQLCipher, WorkManager,
AlarmManager, Glance widget, Quick Settings tile, Android Keystore, Google Tink, ML Kit (on-device text recognition),
Gradle with three modules. About 14,300 non-blank lines of Kotlin in 85 files, of which about 2,100 are tests.

Architecture (checklist items 1 and 2): the hard logic lives in a pure JVM module that is forbidden, by a build rule, to
import anything from Android, so it is tested without a phone. A separate command-line module generates training
data, trains the date-role model and reports accuracy. The Android module only displays state and talks to the system.

Algorithmic depth (item 1):
- Scheduling as a flow problem: Dinic max-flow with preemptive deadlines, a minimal-peak levelling pass, latest safe
  start by binary search over re-solves, and a min-cut explanation of any shortfall with concrete remedies. Checked
  against an independent Hall-condition oracle.
- A rule-based temporal tagger with explicit ambiguity flags, a calibrated softmax classifier that decides whether a
  date is a deadline, an event, a start date, a publication date or history, and relation linking that recognises
  updates, cancellations and duplicates.
- A reminder planner with an attention budget, quiet hours and hysteresis on urgency states.
- Repeating dates computed from the original date so they never drift (31st of a month, 29 February).

Platform integration: an opt-in notification listener (allowlist of 16 chat and mail apps, on-device extraction, no
notification text stored, a consent screen before Android's own access list), and a custom notification bar dashboard
built from RemoteViews with a progress ring, theme-aware cards and lock-screen privacy.

Security engineering: a key hierarchy (Keystore-wrapped master, HKDF-derived keys per purpose), an encrypted database,
end-to-end encrypted backups in a bounded, chunked XChaCha20-Poly1305 container with a recovery key and a snapshot
chain, a tamper-evident HMAC hash chain over each deadline's history, no network permission (checked on every
build), and Android Auto Backup switched off.

Testing (item 4): 234 tests with no failures: 180 unit tests on the core, including adversarial tests that alter every byte of
a backup, truncate, reorder and splice it, and 54 app tests (UI render tests that draw the main screens in all four themes on a
simulated Android and write images for visual review and check that each screen composes, not pixel differences; tests
of the notification reader, the notification bar dashboard (drawn in every theme) and the readable history; and 5 tests of
hostile input to the store, importer and deep links); an evaluation harness for the date reader (95.8% role accuracy and no
false prompts on a held-out set of 71 mentions, from a same-author gold set so the figure is optimistic); lint and a
build pipeline script that checks the merged manifest, signing and style rules. Store and reminder logic in the app layer
still needs its own tests.

Documented problem solving (item 5): the evaluation harness exposed three date-reading bugs ("Rent for November" read as
a month window, "Black Friday" read as a weekday, a weekday that did not match its date); lint caught a crash on
Android 8 caused by a StrongBox call that needs Android 9; a library had quietly added the INTERNET permission, which
contradicted the privacy claim and is now removed explicitly.

Honest limits: the app layer has not been run on a physical phone. Reminder delivery depends on each phone maker's
battery rules and needs real-device testing. The date reader understands English text only.

3. FUTURE SCOPE AND UPGRADE OPPORTUNITIES

Realistic next steps, in a sensible order:
- Version 1.1: real-device test pass on two or three phone brands; localisation of the interface
  (dates already follow the phone's language, wording does not); a second language for the date reader, starting with
  the languages ML Kit text recognition supports.
- Wear OS or notification actions for completing a task from a watch.
- Smarter planning: learn the user's real working hours from logged progress, and offer to split a task around a
  fixed commitment such as a lecture or a shift.
- Calendar read-only import (with the user's consent) to see free time when planning.
- Version 2, which needs a server and is why it was left out of version 1: friends and groups with shared
  repeating deadlines, in-app chat, and GIF replies. This requires a network permission, in-app reporting and
  blocking and moderation to meet Google Play's user-generated-content rules, and a GIF provider (Tenor's API closed in
  June 2026, so GIPHY or KLIPY). Privacy would be kept by end-to-end encrypting group content.
- Optional, user-triggered smart reading of long documents with a small on-device language model, kept off by default.
- An open, documented export format for the encrypted backup so other tools can read it with the user's key.
