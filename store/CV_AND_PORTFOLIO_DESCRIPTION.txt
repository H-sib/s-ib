S-IB: DESCRIPTION FOR YOUR CV AND PORTFOLIO
Written for H-sib (Chowdhury Hasibur Rahaman). Every number below was measured from this project on 2026-10-14.
Copy what you need. Section 7 says what to be careful about when you speak about it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE ONE LINE
S-IB is a privacy-first Android app that finds deadlines in the things you already receive, works out the latest day
you can safely start, and reminds you early and calmly, all on the phone with no internet permission.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PORTFOLIO PARAGRAPH (about 90 words)
Deadlines get lost in the middle: a date is announced, weeks pass, and it arrives as a surprise. S-IB reads a pasted
message, a screenshot, a PDF or a shared file on the phone, suggests the dates it finds and explains why, and only
adds what you confirm. It plans the work backwards from each date using a scheduling model, says exactly how short
you are when something cannot fit, and sends a small number of well-timed reminders. It also has a private space,
Close ones, for anniversaries and birthdays. Nothing leaves the phone, and backups are end-to-end encrypted.

3. CV BULLETS (pick four to six)
- Designed, built and prepared for release a Kotlin and Jetpack Compose Android app (S-IB) as a solo developer:
  about 13,000 lines of Kotlin across a pure-JVM core engine, an evaluation tool and the app, with 218 automated
  tests (180 on the core engine, 33 UI render tests and 5 hostile-input tests).
- Built a deadline planner that models work as a flow network (Dinic max-flow) to spread effort across days, find the
  latest safe start by binary search, and explain a shortfall with a minimum cut plus concrete remedies; verified
  against an independent Hall-condition oracle in tests.
- Wrote a rule-based multi-format date reader with ambiguity flags (day/month order, midnight, missing year, AM/PM)
  and a small softmax model, trained on generated data, that decides whether a date is a deadline, an event, a start
  date, a publication date or history; temperature-calibrated, and measured with an evaluation harness
  (95.8% role accuracy and zero false prompts on a 71-mention held-out set, with the caveat in section 7).
- Engineered a privacy-first security design: no network permission, an encrypted database (SQLCipher) keyed through
  the Android Keystore, per-purpose keys derived with HKDF, and end-to-end encrypted backups using chunked
  XChaCha20-Poly1305 with a 256-bit recovery key; the backup parser is tested against tampering of every byte,
  truncation, reordering and splicing.
- Added a tamper-evident history (an HMAC hash chain, "Provenance Seal") so any unrecorded change to a deadline can
  be detected.
- Built reminders that respect attention: a daily budget, quiet hours, lock-screen privacy, recovery after reboot and
  time-zone change, and an in-app reminder health check; plus a home-screen widget (Glance), a Quick Settings tile
  and a share target that accepts text, images, PDFs, calendar files, chat exports, Word and Excel files.
- Created a distinctive, accessible interface (four themes (Light, Twilight, Black and Neon) with graded colour, the Sora typeface, shape-coded urgency so colour is
  never the only signal, all text contrast pairs at WCAG AA or better) and a second themed space for personal dates.

4. THE LONGER DESCRIPTION

The problem
People are told dates in notices, group chats, emails and screenshots. By the time a date matters, it has been buried.
Calendar apps need you to type everything in, and most "smart" apps want your inbox and send your data to a server.

What S-IB does
- Capture without typing: type one line, paste, share from any app, take a photo, scan a page, or hand over a PDF, a
  Word or Excel file, a calendar file or a chat export. Everything is read on the phone (on-device text recognition).
- Suggest, never assume: found dates wait in a Review inbox with the sentence they came from, the reason each date
  was taken or ignored, and a warning when a reading is uncertain. Publication dates, history and sale offers are
  ignored. Nothing becomes a deadline until the user confirms it.
- Plan backwards: each deadline has an effort estimate (three-point, learned from how long the user's past jobs took).
  The planner spreads work over the days the user has, shows the latest safe start, and when the load does not fit it
  says by how much and offers three remedies: more time per day, a later deadline, or less scope.
- Remind with restraint: few reminders, each placed with care, never in quiet hours, with Done and Snooze built in.
- Close ones: a private space with its own rose-and-plum theme for anniversaries, birthdays, date nights and gifts.
  Occasions repeat without drifting, remind early enough to plan, and never show names or titles on the lock screen.
  They are left out of the widget, summary, calendar export and sharing.
- Share without a server: a deadline or a whole repeating series can be sent to friends as a standard calendar file
  plus a readable list; no account is needed.
- Safe by design: encrypted storage, encrypted backup to any folder the user chooses (including a cloud drive), and a
  tamper-evident history.

Architecture
- Three Gradle modules. ":core" is pure Kotlin on the JVM and a build rule forbids Android imports in it, which keeps
  the hard logic testable without a phone. ":eval" is a command-line tool that generates training data, trains the
  model and reports accuracy. ":app" is Android: Compose UI, Room with SQLCipher, WorkManager, AlarmManager, Glance.
- Time is treated carefully: a deadline stores the local time and the zone it was stated in, not a bare instant, and
  repeating dates are always computed from the original date so the 31st or 29 February never drifts.

Engineering decisions worth discussing
- No INTERNET permission on purpose, removed explicitly from the merged manifest, so the privacy claim can be checked
  by anyone reading the manifest.
- Argon2 was avoided for passphrase exports because of a library conflict on Android; PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 at 600,000
  iterations is used instead and the choice is documented.
- Android's Auto Backup is switched off so private data cannot leave through a path the user does not control.
- Reminders use inexact allow-while-idle alarms and a health check rather than the exact-alarm permission, which Play
  restricts; the trade-off is explained to the user in the app.

6. THE NAME
Say it as "sib". It is the closing sound of Hasib, a name that can mean one who counts or reckons, and counting the days
is what the app does. It also stands for Start It Before, the whole idea of the product. In English a sib is a
brother or sister, which is the spirit of the Close ones space and the sharing features planned for a later version.
Before using the name publicly, check trademarks in your markets: SIB is also the abbreviation of several banks,
which is why the store title is "S-IB: Deadline Planner".

7. BE CAREFUL ABOUT THESE (so you can answer any interviewer honestly)
- The accuracy figures come from a test set of 133 lines written by the same person who wrote the training data
  generator, scored on a held-out half (71 date mentions). They show the reader behaves as designed; they are not a
  claim about real-world messages.
- The core engine is thoroughly tested by machine (180 tests). The app layer has been compiled, linted and rendered on a
  simulated Android in tests (33), but, as of this writing, has not been run on a physical phone. Reminder delivery depends on the phone maker's
  battery rules and must be tested on real devices before you claim it works everywhere.
- Groups, chat and GIFs were designed but deliberately left for version 2: they need a server, a network permission
  and Play's user-generated-content rules. Do not describe them as shipped.
- Say "designed and built solo with an AI coding assistant" if asked how it was built; the design decisions, scope and
  review were yours.
- Do not say it is "unbreakable" or "military grade". Say what it does: encrypted at rest, end-to-end encrypted
  backups, no network permission, tamper-evident history, and the limits stated in the app's Security screen.
